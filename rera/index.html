RERA complaints — delayed possession, refund, compensation

Real-estate complaints go to your state RERA authority — not to e-Jagriti. The portal is picked by where the property is, not where you live. This route only exists for RERA-registered projects; for an unregistered project, the remedy is the Consumer Commission or a civil court.

Limitation: Generally 3 years from the cause of action (Limitation Act, Article 137, as applied by the courts — the RERA Act itself does not fix a period). File sooner rather than later.

Interest: Typically SBI MCLR + 2% per year. State rules differ — for example, Uttar Pradesh rules link interest to SBI MCLR + 1%. Check your state portal for the rate it applies.

Appeal: Either side may appeal to the Real Estate Appellate Tribunal within 60 days of receiving the order (Section 44).

What you can claim (Section 18)

If the promoter fails to complete the project or hand over possession by the date agreed in the agreement for sale, you have an unqualified right to either stay and claim interest for the delay, or withdraw and take a full refund of everything you paid, with interest. The builder may not deduct anything from a Section 18 refund. If the builder ignores the order, the amount is recoverable as arrears of land revenue (Section 40).

Frequently asked questions

Should I file with the RERA Authority or the Adjudicating Officer?
Refund, interest or possession go to the RERA Authority (Section 31, usually Form M). Compensation for loss — beyond interest — goes to the Adjudicating Officer (Section 71, usually Form N). Many homebuyers file both.
What is the RERA complaint fee?
₹1,000 in most states and ₹5,000 in Maharashtra for the Authority; usually ₹5,000 for the Adjudicating Officer. Tamil Nadu charges ₹600 postage in addition. Confirm the fee on your state portal before paying.
How long does a RERA complaint take?
The disposal target is 60 days for both the Authority and the Adjudicating Officer. In practice many complaints take longer; if the target is missed, the forum must record its reasons.
